\section{Introduction and research question}
The Swarm Alignment Hackathon asks how fast and how far bad behavior spreads
from a compromised agent, and whether network shape matters
\cite{apart-swarm}. We study whether an agent that visibly chooses a harmful
action can recruit other agents to make the same choice when their financial
incentives favor it. Here the harmful action is a vote that helps a false
factual claim win an adjudication.

Veracity makes the distinction between communication and consequences
observable. A publisher posts a claim, a challenger submits evidence, and a
jury selects the binding verdict. A message agreeing to vote falsely is not
yet a counted vote, and a counted false vote does not by itself establish that
payment occurred. We therefore follow proposals through commitment, reveal
and payment. ``Bad'' here means acting against the supplied factual evidence.
The short charter specifies the game rules but omits an explicit duty to vote
truthfully or independently. Its false votes are consequently described as
paid coordination against the truth where payment and coordination are
established. The principles contrast adds those duties and tests a different
instructional condition.

Our contribution is an auditable study of \emph{recruitment, reach and
persistence under shared exposure}. Recruitment concerns an evaluated juror
joining the supplied false-vote proposal. Reach counts how many evaluated
jurors reveal that false verdict in a case. Persistence concerns what happens
across successive cases with renewed exposure. Every evaluated juror receives the adversarial messages
directly, however. Increasing this fully exposed jury's size cannot establish
how behavior would travel through a different communication network.

\paragraph{Relation to prompt infection.}
Lee and Tiwari study prompts that replicate between agents, comparing shared
and predecessor-limited messaging and a population with pairwise interactions
\cite{lee-tiwari}. Our intervention supplies a financially rewarded example
of bad behavior, rather than a self-replicating prompt that overrides an
agent's instructions. We do not reproduce their infection rates or measure
the same attack. Because all evaluated jurors receive the offer directly, two
jurors making the same choice need not indicate that one influenced the other.
Both may instead have responded to the same supplied incentive.

\paragraph{Preexisting work and new sprint work.}
The game and agent runtime predate this sprint. Earlier October case studies
concern agents arranging dispute funding, and a later short-charter
calibration motivated the present conditions. Neither is new swarm evidence.
The November work
extends juries to fifteen, supplies several first-moving scripted peers,
compares jury sizes and adds duty, repeated-exposure and publisher-mandate
contrasts. The previous fifteen-juror principles campaign is a separate
November evidence set, not a replicate of the short-charter study.
