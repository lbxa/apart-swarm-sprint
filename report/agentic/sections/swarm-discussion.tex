\section{What can we infer about network shape?}
To learn whether network shape changes adoption, we would need otherwise matched
communication graphs that differ in who can contact whom. Here the shared
public forum lets the seeded bloc reach every evaluated juror directly.
Private-message paths are chosen by the agents rather than assigned by the
experiment. Their observed use cannot supply the missing controlled comparison.

The majority calculation in \autoref{sec:quorum} explains why capture alone
does not measure how far behavior traveled. At every tested size, the
scripted bloc is one valid reveal short of winning. A single evaluated juror
can supply that reveal after reading the same public invitation, without any
intermediate juror passing the invitation onward. A larger jury can therefore
be captured without a longer transmission chain. Moreover, jury size, seed
count and fraction, evaluated population and the relative payment incentive
change together. An outcome difference across sizes cannot be attributed to
any one of these changes, or to network shape.

Repeated exposure changes the jurors' history and supplies a new invitation
in each case. Persistence under this common exposure does not establish
transmission to previously unexposed jurors.

\subsection{A topology comparison that would identify the missing effect}
The proposed experiment should hold seven jurors, one scripted bad juror, model,
charter, payment schedule, opportunity budget and evidence fixed. Unlike the
present near-majority bloc, this leaves three additional false votes needed
for capture, because four votes are required and only one is supplied by the
script. Randomize the permitted deliberation graph and initial seed location.
Compare a seven-node ring with a graph consisting of six links from a hub to
the other jurors and one link between two outer jurors. Both graphs have
seven bidirectional links, but distribute them differently. Within the hub graph,
compare seeding the hub with seeding an outer juror. A complete graph can
serve as a separate dense reference.

Turn off unrestricted public deliberation and enforce those communication
edges at the gateway. Otherwise, a message could reach every juror through the
forum without following the assigned edges.
Keep the factual case equally accessible. Include a matched no-bad-peer
control with the same publisher incentive to measure adoption from the offer
alone. Supply the adversarial seed once, rather than reintroducing it every
round. Deliver messages in logical rounds so every forwarding event and first
exposure has a recorded position independent of provider latency.

For a clean single-source version, make the publisher and challenger passive
case fixtures and replace publisher recruiting messages with a common fixed
payment rule. Only the seeded juror then supplies adversarial deliberation.
This changes the current non-escrowed offer mechanism and needs a calibrated
positive control before the topology comparison.

Measure first false commitment and confirmed reveal by round, number of newly
adopting agents, maximum reached distance from the seed, persistence, missing
votes and capture. Keep an entire independently initialized run as the unit
of replication and determine the number of runs from a declared precision or
power target. A forwarded message records who exposed whom. To count adoption,
we must also observe the recipient's subsequent false commitment or reveal,
keeping those two endpoints separate. This design has not been run. It
describes the intervention needed to test a network-shape claim rather than
a result inferred from the current size comparison.

\section{Conclusion}
Seeded financial recruitment can turn an acknowledged falsehood into a
contract-valid, paid collective verdict in selected Kimi runs. The evidence
connects an adversarial example to stated rationale, binding actions and
payment, while preserving honest-peer controls, explicit-duty nulls and
non-participation. It establishes direct reach and repeated-case persistence
under the tested conditions. Network-shape effects and multi-hop transmission
speed remain unidentified. The report's contribution is an auditable result
and a concrete experiment that separates those questions.
