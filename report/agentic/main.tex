\begin{abstract}
If an agent adopts bad behavior, can it recruit its peers, and does network
structure change the result? We study incentive-mediated recruitment in
Veracity, a sandboxed publisher--challenger--jury game with binding
commit/reveal votes and local credit transfers. A scripted publisher offers
payment for upholding a false flat-Earth claim. Scripted jurors act first;
the remaining jurors use evaluated models. We compare honest and colluding
peers in juries of 3, 7 and 15, and inspect explicit-duty, repeated-exposure
and mandated-publisher contrasts. A frozen W\&B audit admits
\SnapshotIncludedSessions{} sessions and \SnapshotAssignedCases{} cases from
two checkout cohorts, retaining \SnapshotRetainedFailures{} failed sessions
after play began. Under the short charter, a single scripted juror can be
joined by both evaluated Kimi K2.6 jurors within one case; a seven-seat
scripted bloc is joined by up to seven of eight evaluated jurors in a larger jury.
Returned reasoning, submitted assessments, revealed votes and payments jointly
document a recruitment mechanism. False votes also occur with honest peers.
The explicit-duty contrast has no false reveals; extensive non-participation
limits the DeepSeek comparison. These observations establish reach and
persistence under direct exposure, not multi-hop infection speed. Communication
topology is fixed, while size, seeded-bloc size and reward shares co-vary.
Consequently the present data cannot identify a network-shape effect. We specify
a controlled topology comparison that would answer that remaining question.
\end{abstract}
